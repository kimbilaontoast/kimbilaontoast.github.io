I rise before dawn.

I breathe in the beauty of the crisp morning air, letting it fill my lungs and course through my body. The rising light in the forest bathes the rocks in a blush of purple and ochre. This is my country, this is Country, and I am connected to the land and this place. 

I begin the day with movement, stirring the body to life, walking meditation. If the mind needs sharpening I recite the Dhamma, as contemplating the Dhamma awakens the mind. I write, and let words flow from me from some unseen place of wonder. I sit meditation in the morning, when the mind is awakened, and cultivate stillness and an open heart. Meditation is the foundation of everything.  

I serve the community with all my heart, knowing that the activities I do are for the benefit of the community, as well as myself. *Nibbāna paccayo hotu* - may this be the cause of Nibbāna - this is my mantra. I keep this in mind as I work, knowing that the service I give brightens the world around me. I give my work everything I've got.

I eat one meal a day, in moderation; knowing I eat simply to nourish the body. This keeps the body light, and strong.

I wear this robe and take care of my requisites with pride, knowing that the robe is the banner of the Arahants, and the bowl my whole livelihood, a mendicant. I am this light, knowing that I can walk this world with just this much; the teachings in my heart, and the robes and bowl as my companions. 

I speak well and think well of the people around me, including those I find difficult, as there is always good in people, and I choose to see that. I cultivate harmony in the community by being considerate of others, being humble enough to listen, and doing as others wish to do. I respect others, without expectation, and live as a monastic who is truthful and straight, someone who is worthy of trust, a friend.

I listen to the word of the Buddha, cherishing the teachings, and reflect on the miracle in coming across something so precious as the Dhamma in this human life. Each day I connect with the Dhamma, I read suttas, study the vinaya, study Pāli. With patience and persistence, I slowly build my understanding, bringing this teaching deep into my heart, so that I may become this path, and gift it to others. 

I sit meditation, and feel the stillness emerging in the mind; the raw power and beauty of samādhi, the promise of awakening. The path inside the present is always with me, waiting to be seen. I endure any hardships I encounter, with the soft, brave spirit of a true man; one who opens his heart in love.

When the night falls, I sit, in silence, and witness the dark enveloping the forest, as the world slip under a smooth wave of silken black. I remember all the good I have done, the moments of connection and beauty in this day, all the good I see in myself and others. Holding my heart, I ask what things I must let go, in order to be free. 

I sleep, in the faith I will awaken.
